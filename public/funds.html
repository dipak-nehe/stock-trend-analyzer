What these measures mean
Expense ratio
The share of your money the fund takes each year to run itself. It's the one thing you know in advance, and over decades it adds up: see cost over 20 years.
Cost over 20 years
What the expense ratio takes from $10,000 growing 7% a year for 20 years. 7% is a round illustration, not a forecast.
Returns
Average total return a year over 1, 5 and 10 years, after the fund's costs and without any sales charge, as the fund reports them.
Against its index
The fund's 10-year return minus its index's (its own benchmark when the report names one, otherwise the broad market). An index fund should trail by about its costs; an active fund is trying to beat it.
Net assets
The fund's size, for all its share classes together.
Top 10 holdings
The fund's ten largest holdings and how much of it sits in them, a measure of concentration, from its latest holdings report to the SEC (Form N-PORT, public about two months after each quarter). Share classes of one company (Alphabet A and C) and one issuer's bonds count as one holding; futures, swaps and cash are left out.
Turnover
How much of the portfolio the fund bought and sold in the year. High turnover means more trading costs, which aren't in the expense ratio, and often more taxes in a taxable account.

From each fund's latest annual shareholder report (Form N-CSR) on SEC EDGAR, or the half-year report when there's no recent annual one (it has no returns). Unit investment trusts such as SPY and DIA don't file these reports, so they can't be shown. No prices: look them up on the fund company's site.
