Terms explained
CAGR
Compound annual growth rate: the steady yearly growth that would turn the first value into the latest one.
EPS (diluted)
Earnings per share: net income divided by the number of shares, counting options and convertibles.
Payout ratio
The share of net income paid out as dividends.
Operating cash flow
Cash the business actually generated from its operations in the year.
Free cash flow
Operating cash flow minus capital spending (new equipment, buildings): the cash left for dividends, buybacks or debt.
Current ratio
Current assets divided by current liabilities: can the company pay bills due within a year?
Debt / equity
Total debt divided by shareholders' equity: how much the company borrows relative to what shareholders own.
Interest coverage
Operating income divided by interest expense: how many times profits cover the interest bill.
ROE
Return on equity: net income as a percentage of shareholders' equity.
P/E and P/B
Share price divided by earnings per share, or by book value per share. Lower means you pay less for each dollar of profit or assets.
Graham Number
√(22.5 × EPS × book value per share): the highest price Benjamin Graham would pay under his defensive rules.
Margin of safety
How far the price sits below an estimate of value, as a cushion against mistakes in the estimate.
Goodwill
The amount paid for acquisitions above the value of their assets; written off if a deal disappoints.
Dilution / buybacks
Issuing new shares shrinks each shareholder's slice; buying shares back grows it.
10-K, 20-F, 8-K
SEC filings: the annual report (US companies), the annual report (foreign companies), and a report of a major event.
